History of the Internet

The Internet began as a small research project connecting a handful of university computers and grew into the global infrastructure that now underpins communication, commerce, and daily life for billions of people. What started as ARPANET in 1969 evolved through the invention of TCP/IP and DNS, the birth of the World Wide Web, the dot-com boom and bust, the rise of social media and cloud computing, and the more recent emergence of mobile, 5G, and AI-integrated networks. This page traces that evolution through key events and the technologies that made each stage possible.

Timeline of Key Events

October 29, 1969 — First ARPANET Message

UCLA student programmer Charley Kline sent the first host-to-host message over ARPANET to Bill Duvall at the Stanford Research Institute (SRI). This demonstrated that geographically separated computers could exchange data reliably.

Source: Computer History Museum

January 1, 1983 — ARPANET Adopts TCP/IP

ARPANET completed its transition from NCP to TCP/IP, allowing independent networks to communicate through a common protocol — a key step toward the modern Internet.

Source: Internet Society

November 1983 — Domain Name System (DNS) Invented

RFC 882 defined DNS: a scalable, hierarchical naming system, replacing a single manually maintained list of host names.

Source: IETF (RFC 882)

1986 — NSFNET Backbone Launched

The U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) funded NSFNET, a 56 Kbps backbone connecting supercomputing centers and regional research networks. This scaled the network from thousands to millions of connected hosts.

Source: U.S. National Science Foundation

March 1989 — Tim Berners-Lee Proposes the World Wide Web

While working at CERN, Tim Berners-Lee circulated a proposal for a distributed hypertext system to help researchers share information. The idea combined hypertext with the Internet, laying the groundwork for HTTP, HTML, and the URL.

Source: CERN

August 6, 1991 — The World Wide Web Goes Public

Berners-Lee published the first website and made the WorldWideWeb software available to the public, introducing the concepts of web pages, hyperlinks, and browsers that define the Web today.

Source: CERN

April 30, 1993 — CERN Puts the Web in the Public Domain

CERN released the World Wide Web technology royalty-free, removing licensing barriers and enabling anyone to build on the Web without paying fees.

Source: CERN

1993 — Mosaic Browser Released

The NCSA Mosaic browser brought a graphical, easy-to-use interface to the Web, including inline images, and is widely credited with sparking mainstream public interest in the Internet.

Source: Living Internet

1994–1995 — Commercial Internet Takes Off

Netscape Navigator popularized web browsing, while Amazon (1994) and eBay (1995) launched as some of the earliest commercial websites, signaling the Internet's shift from a research tool to a consumer marketplace.

Source: Internet Society

April 30, 1995 — NSFNET Backbone Decommissioned

As commercial Internet Service Providers (ISPs) expanded, NSF funding ended, marking the shift toward a commercial, publicly accessible Internet.

Source: Internet Society

September 1998 — Google Founded

Larry Page and Sergey Brin founded Google, whose PageRank algorithm dramatically improved search relevance and reshaped how people navigated the growing Web.

Source: Google

2000–2001 — Dot-Com Bubble Bursts

A speculative boom in Internet-based companies collapsed, wiping out trillions in market value, but the surviving infrastructure and companies laid the foundation for the next phase of Web growth.

Source: Investopedia

2004–2006 — Rise of Web 2.0 and Social Media

Platforms such as Facebook (2004), YouTube (2005), and Twitter (2006) turned the Web into an interactive, user-generated medium, a shift often labeled "Web 2.0."

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica

2006 — Cloud Computing Goes Mainstream

Amazon Web Services launched S3 and EC2, popularizing on-demand cloud infrastructure and letting businesses rent computing power instead of owning data centers.

Source: Amazon Web Services

June 29, 2007 — The iPhone Launches Mobile Internet

Apple's iPhone made full web browsing practical on a pocket device, accelerating the shift of Internet use from desktops to smartphones worldwide.

Source: Apple Newsroom

June 6, 2012 — World IPv6 Launch

Major ISPs, home networking manufacturers, and web companies permanently enabled IPv6, addressing the exhaustion of the older IPv4 address space and preparing the Internet for billions of new connected devices.

Source: Internet Society

2013 — Global Surveillance Disclosures

Edward Snowden's disclosures about government surveillance programs sparked a worldwide debate about online privacy, encryption, and the balance between security and civil liberties on the Internet.

Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica

2019–2020 — 5G Rollout and the COVID-19 Surge

Commercial 5G networks began rolling out globally just as the COVID-19 pandemic pushed remote work, online learning, telehealth, and video conferencing into daily life for billions of people, straining and reshaping Internet infrastructure.

Source: International Telecommunication Union (ITU)

November 30, 2022 — Generative AI Reaches the Mainstream

OpenAI's release of ChatGPT brought conversational generative AI to a mass audience, kicking off rapid adoption of AI chatbots and assistants across search, productivity, and content creation on the Web.

Source: OpenAI

2023–2026 — The AI-Integrated Internet

AI assistants and agents became embedded directly into browsers, search engines, and productivity tools, while debates over data privacy, AI-generated content, and platform regulation continued to shape how the Internet is governed and used today.

Source: Internet Society

Key Protocols and Technologies

Protocol / Technology Introduced Purpose
NCP (Network Control Protocol) 1970 Original ARPANET host-to-host protocol, later replaced by TCP/IP
TCP/IP 1983 Core communication protocol suite enabling networks of networks
DNS 1983 Translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses
HTTP 1990–1991 Protocol for transferring hypertext (web pages) between clients and servers
HTML 1990–1991 Markup language used to structure and format web pages
SSL/TLS 1995 / 1999 Encrypts data in transit, enabling secure ("https") communication
Wi-Fi (802.11) 1997 Wireless local-area networking standard
IPv6 1998 (spec) / 2012 (launch) Expanded address space to accommodate billions of connected devices
4G LTE 2009 Mobile network standard fast enough to rival fixed broadband
HTTP/2 2015 Sped up page loading by multiplexing requests over one connection
5G 2019 High-speed, low-latency mobile standard supporting massive device density
HTTP/3 (QUIC) 2022 Further reduces latency by running HTTP over a faster, UDP-based transport
Low-Earth-Orbit Satellite Internet 2019–present Delivers broadband to rural and remote areas unreached by fiber or cable
AI-Driven Network Infrastructure 2023–present Edge computing and traffic optimization built to handle AI workloads at scale
