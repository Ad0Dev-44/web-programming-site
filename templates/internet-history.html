Timeline

October 29, 1969 — First ARPANET Message

UCLA student programmer Charley Kline sent the first host-to-host message over ARPANET to Bill Duvall at the Stanford Research Institute (SRI). This demonstrated that geographically separated computers could exchange data reliably.

Source: Computer History Museum

January 1, 1983 — ARPANET Adopts TCP/IP

ARPANET completed its transition from NCP to TCP/IP, allowing independent networks to communicate through a common protocol — a key step toward the modern Internet.

Source: Internet Society

November 1983 — Domain Name System (DNS) Invented

RFC 882 defined DNS: a scalable, hierarchical naming system, replacing a single manually maintained list of host names.

Source: IETF (RFC 882)

1986 — NSFNET Backbone Launched

The U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) funded NSFNET, a 56 Kbps backbone connecting supercomputing centers and regional research networks.This scaled the network from thousands to millions of connected hosts.

Source: U.S. National Science Foundation

April 30, 1995 — NSFNET Backbone Decommissioned

As commercial Internet Service Providers (ISPs) expanded, NSF funding ended, marking the shift toward a commercial, publicly accessible Internet.

Source: Internet Society
